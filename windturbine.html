Kidwind Wind Turbine

The Kidwind Wind Turbine is a competition across schools in Northern California that is intended to teach kids the basics of clean energy, and for the competiton I worked on and created our unique wind turbine for was a wind energy competition. The challenge was to generate the highest watts when faced with wind blowing into the turbine.


https://kidwind.org/events/in-person-challenge/


The competition involved a lot of freedom in your design, so what we primarily chose to focus on was the design of the blades, for we felt this had the greatest impact on the energy generated. We used a software called Whitebox Learning which showed us in-depth models on what each part of the blade contributed to, like the root, chord, and the tip of the blade. We determined that the longer the chord of the blade the higher the yield of the energy would be. Just like the chord, the longer the root meant more power, per the simulation.


We learned a lot throughout the process of the project, including the terminology of all things wind turbine, how much engineering and time truly goes into these massive machines, and how to take into consideration the constraints and aspects of the engineering process.

Unfortunately during our part competing at the event, our turbine did not perform very well due to a faulty stopper for our gear, so as soon as the fan started blowing the gear moved down the axle and stopped moving the generator's gear. We later put a new stopper on and with it the turbine worked well, but it was too late to test again.
